01 · Overview

A census of the evidence behind every active spaceflight risk.

NASA grades every crew-health risk on a Level of Evidence, from low Earth orbit to Mars. Project CASCADE is the first study to read every Risk Summary Package the Board approved from 2020 to 2025, across all 29 active human-system risks, and ask whether the evidence inside actually matches the grade.

Read
A dual-path pipeline ingests the standardized risk tables and runs natural-language processing over package text and embedded images.
Classify
Every cited piece of evidence is sorted into the framework's eight evidence types, checked against an expert-annotated gold standard.
Compare
The documented evidence is tested against the assigned grade across the whole portfolio and along the mission gradient.
Map
The risks and evidence types are modeled as a network to surface shared-evidence communities, hubs, and connections.

What the analysis found

Across the portfolio, the amount, quality, and diversity of documented evidence did not, on their own, predict a risk's Level of Evidence. The clearer signal is at the mission resolution: the grade weakened systematically from low Earth orbit toward Mars, and evidence breadth thinned along the same axis. That mission gradient, not any cross-risk comparison, is the stronger result.

How a risk is scored

What the score means, and why Project CASCADE checks it

For every risk, NASA records two things side by side: how serious the risk is judged to be, and how much confidence the underlying evidence warrants. That second judgment is the Level of Evidence. Project CASCADE focuses on it, asking whether the grade a risk receives lines up with the evidence actually documented for it.

The Level of Evidence scale
1 · Strong
Direct, consistent evidence firmly establishes the hazard-to-outcome link.
2 · Moderate
Good supporting evidence, with some gaps or reliance on indirect sources.
3 · Weak
Limited or mixed evidence; the link is plausible but uncertain.
4 · Speculative
Little direct evidence; the link is largely hypothesized.

A plain-language guide to NASA's Level-of-Evidence scale (JSC risk management plan). Lower numbers mean stronger, more certain evidence.

How the grade is reached
1
Document
Evidence for the risk is gathered in a standardized Risk Summary Package.
2
Grade
Each risk is given a Level of Evidence, judged separately for In-Mission Operations and Long-Term Health and across mission profiles from low Earth orbit to Mars.
3
Decide
A likelihood-by-consequence posture, summarized red / yellow / green, and a disposition that ranges from accepting the risk to requiring active mitigation set the agency's response.
02 · Research

One question, pursued through three aims.

“

How do the types, sources, and volume of human-spaceflight risk evidence documented in NASA HSRB risk summary packages (across the twenty-nine active human-system risks and ninety-three approved submissions spanning 2020 to 2025) align with the Board's Level-of-Evidence scoring and risk-disposition decisions across Design Reference Missions, and what do the structural properties of the resulting risk–evidence network reveal about the organization, connectivity, and evidence-based buydown capacity of NASA's spaceflight human-health risk architecture?

AIM 01

Evidence Extraction & Level-of-Evidence Evaluation

A systematic review of the risk summary packages using natural-language processing to extract, classify, and quantify the documented evidence, then evaluate how it aligns with the Board's Level-of-Evidence scoring across all twenty-nine risks.

AIM 02

Network Topological Characterization

Characterizing the network topology of the portfolio and of two contrasting-complexity risks, using degree, betweenness, clustering, and a full bipartite motif analysis to surface hub risks, bridging evidence types, and recurring structural patterns.

AIM 03

Thematic Synthesis & Recommendations

Synthesizing the quantitative outputs through a deductive thematic analysis of how evidence is documented across the packages, consolidating where practice aligns with the scores and where it diverges into data-informed recommendations.

03 · Public health impact

Making the evidence behind the score visible.

Why it matters

As missions extend toward sustained lunar operations and interplanetary transit, the margin for evidentiary uncertainty narrows, and crews days or months from definitive medical care depend on risk decisions built on a transparent, well-characterized evidence base. Project CASCADE offers a reproducible way to see how that evidence is documented and graded across the whole portfolio, a contribution to the transparency of evidence evaluation rather than a claim about any single safety decision.

A public health contribution

Project CASCADE makes visible, across the whole portfolio under one consistent standard, where the evidence is strong, where it is thin, and where documentation diverges from the scores it supports. It establishes an empirical baseline against which future revisions to the evidence base can be measured.

Scientific & operational relevance

The study contributes a reproducible computational-epidemiology methodology for evaluating expert-graded evidence at the scale of an entire risk portfolio, combining systematic NLP extraction, classification against a published evidence hierarchy, and the tools of network medicine (centrality, community detection, and motif analysis) to map how evidence is produced, weighted, and connected. The result is a transparent, repeatable framework rather than a one-off review, one that can be re-run as new evidence is approved and extended to any future portfolio that must grade evidence under uncertainty.

Transferable beyond spaceflight

The same approach works anywhere experts must grade evidence when good data are scarce. In occupational epidemiology, it could audit how exposure and health evidence is weighed when workplace safety standards are set. In extreme-condition risk management, from deep-sea diving and polar expeditions to military and disaster medicine, it could check whether the evidence on record actually matches the risk judgments that crews and planners rely on. Wherever mixed evidence has to be appraised, graded, and turned into a decision, Project CASCADE can be re-run as a transparent, repeatable audit.

Headline results

What the analysis revealed.

4 communities
the risk–evidence network resolved into four communities, a more modular structure than chance networks of the same size would produce, grouping risks that rest on similar evidence and scoring profiles.
LEO→Mars
the Level of Evidence weakened systematically toward more demanding missions, and evidence breadth thinned along the same axis.
All 29
risks rested on a shared foundation of human-spaceflight and expert-opinion evidence; they differed by volume and concentration, not type.
2 Tracks
A dual-path NLP and structured-extraction pipeline reads every risk package two ways, structured posture tables and free-text evidence mined from PDFs, then triangulates them into one evidence ledger for each risk.

The eight evidence types (JSC‑66705). Types I–VII run strongest→weakest by directness to human spaceflight; VIII is normative and sits outside that ranking. Ⅰ Spaceflight, human: in‑flight crew data (ISS, Shuttle, Apollo, LSAH). Ⅱ Ground‑analog, human: bed‑rest, Antarctic, NEEMO, isolation analogs. Ⅲ Terrestrial clinical & epidemiological: cohorts, trials, registries in the general population. Ⅳ Animal: in‑vivo studies in non‑human animals. Ⅴ Cellular & tissue: in‑vitro, molecular, and tissue studies. Ⅵ Computational & modeling: simulations and risk projections (NSCR, PRA, %REID). Ⅶ Expert opinion: professional judgment or panel consensus not tied to a dataset. Ⅷ Standards & requirements: normative limits and documents (NASA‑STD‑3001, PELs).
