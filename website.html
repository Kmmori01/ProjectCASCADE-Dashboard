01, Overview

A census of the evidence behind every active spaceflight risk.

NASA's Human System Risk Board (HSRB) assigns each crew-health risk a Level of Evidence across a graded series of mission profiles, from low Earth orbit to Mars. Whether the evidence documented in the Board's Risk Summary Packages actually aligns with the Levels of Evidence it assigns had never been examined systematically.

Project CASCADE conducted the first computational analysis of that alignment across the portfolio of active human-system risks, and characterized the structure of the resulting risk-evidence network. A dual-path pipeline combined structured ingestion of the standardized likelihood-by-consequence tables with natural-language processing of package text and embedded images, classifying evidence into the framework's eight evidence types.

What the analysis found

At the portfolio level, evidence volume, quality-weighted composite, and diversity did not predict a risk's worst-case Level of Evidence, indicating that Board scoring integrates mission applicability and deliberative judgment well beyond citation counts. At the mission resolution, the Level of Evidence weakened systematically from low Earth orbit toward Mars, and evidence breadth thinned along the same axis, reflecting a low-Earth-orbit-centric evidence base.

02, Research

One question, pursued through three aims.

“

How do the types, sources, and volume of human-spaceflight risk evidence documented in NASA HSRB risk summary packages, across the twenty-nine active human-system risks and ninety-three approved submissions spanning 2020 to 2025, align with the Board's Level-of-Evidence scoring and risk-disposition decisions across Design Reference Missions, and what do the structural properties of the resulting risk-evidence network reveal about the organization, connectivity, and evidence-based buydown capacity of NASA's spaceflight human-health risk architecture?

AIM 01

Evidence Extraction & Level-of-Evidence Evaluation

A systematic review of the risk summary packages using natural-language processing to extract, classify, and quantify the documented evidence, then evaluate how it aligns with the Board's Level-of-Evidence scoring across all twenty-nine risks.

AIM 02

Multilayer Network Topology Modeling

Modeling the network topology of the portfolio and of two contrasting-complexity risks, using degree, betweenness, clustering, and a full triad census to surface hub risks, bridging evidence types, and recurring structural patterns.

AIM 03

Thematic Synthesis & Recommendations

Synthesizing the quantitative outputs through a deductive thematic analysis of how evidence is documented across the packages, consolidating where practice aligns with the scores and where it diverges into data-informed recommendations.

03, Public health impact

Evidence quality is a determinant of crew safety.

Why it matters

As missions extend toward sustained lunar operations and interplanetary transit, the margin for evidentiary uncertainty narrows. Crews days or months from definitive medical care require risk decisions built on a transparent, defensible evidence foundation, making the consistency of NASA's evidence evaluation a determinant of crew safety rather than a documentary formality.

A public health contribution

CASCADE makes visible, across the whole portfolio under one consistent standard, where the evidence is strong, where it is thin, and where documentation diverges from the scores it supports. It establishes an empirical baseline against which future revisions to the evidence base can be measured.

Scientific & operational relevance

The study contributes a reproducible methodology for evaluating expert-graded evidence at the scale of an entire risk portfolio, combining systematic NLP extraction, classification against a published evidence hierarchy, network analysis, and thematic synthesis, in a form that is transparent and repeatable rather than dependent on any single reviewer.

Transferable beyond spaceflight

The framework transfers to other evidence-based risk-governance systems that grade expert-reviewed evidence under limited data, drug-safety review, occupational-health standard-setting, and pandemic-preparedness assessment, wherever heterogeneous evidence is appraised, graded, and translated into decisions.
